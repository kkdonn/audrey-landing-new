The problem

Two gaps persist in claims operations:
the Money Gap and the Liability Gap.
AI is widening both.

Traditional sampling gives carriers visibility into a fraction of the claims book. Across the rest, financial exposure and decision rationale are harder to see — and AI-driven processing is accelerating the pace.

The Money Gap

Full visibility across the book — not a sample.

Manual QA typically reviews 3–5% of files after they close; AudRI evaluates 100%. Limited sampling can leave leakage, fraud, and reserve errors outside the reviewed population — across the other ~95% of the book.

$6–14MIllustrative recoverable exposure on a $200M claims book — the gap between typical claims leakage (industry benchmarks ~5–10%) and best-in-class handling (~2–3%).
The Liability Gap

Traceable rationale, across every system.

Most carriers already have controls in place. But as AI accelerates decisions, the rationale and authority behind a call can fragment across systems and workflows — and become hard to reconstruct when it's later questioned. AudRI keeps it auditable, traceable, and defensible.

NowPolicyholder attorneys are already developing AI-based bad-faith claims that seek the reasoning behind coverage and valuation decisions (Simpson Thacher, Aug 2026).

What AudRI does

It governs the decision — not just whether a step was completed.

Traditional QA confirms a process step was followed. AudRI governs the decision itself: whether the right amount went to the right party, for a defensible reason, under the appropriate authority — and it preserves that reasoning with the file.

Systems of record store the claim. Dashboards report on it after the fact. AudRI governs the decision at the moment it's made.

Reconstruction & oversight

When a decision is questioned, reconstruct exactly why.

A complete, decision-level reconstruction — the rule, the authority, the evidence, and the human reasoning behind every call.

Human override

Overrides become part of the record.

An adjuster can override any AudRI call — humans retain decision authority. The override, and the rationale behind it, become part of the durable decision record rather than living in memory.

Independence

An independent governance layer.

The system that executes a decision shouldn't be the only system that attests to it. AudRI sits independently across the claim file and decision process — and that independence and cross-file visibility strengthen auditability, regulatory defensibility, and litigation readiness.
